Mouth Sores, White Patches, Peeling Gums — When to See a Dermatologist

Oral mucosa is essentially specialized skin. Cavities and gum disease belong to dentistry — but rashes, blisters, white patches, chronic ulcers, lip inflammation, and pigmented lesions on the mucosa belong to dermatology. This article integrates the 2026 JAAD CME by Klein and Aristizabal, explains which oral symptoms belong to dermatology, what a derm oral exam looks like, when a biopsy is needed, and Taiwan-specific epidemiology of oral lichen planus, recurrent aphthous stomatitis, and betel-related leukoplakia.

Disclaimer: This article is for educational reference only and does not replace face-to-face medical consultation. Oral mucosal lesions require in-person evaluation, with biopsy and pathology when indicated.
